Case study

Redesigning a digital banking experience from the ground up

A banking app that had grown one feature at a time, rebuilt around the few things people open it to do.

Company
Scope
Research synthesis, flows, UI, design system, handoff
Worked with
Design, Engineering, Client stakeholders

Interfaces are reconstructed to respect client confidentiality.

The problem

The bank’s app had grown feature by feature. Each team added its own entry point, and the first screen turned into a catalogue. Customers hunted for routine tasks: checking a balance, paying someone, moving money.

Why it was hard

A regulated product, legacy systems underneath, and features owned by different teams who each wanted the first screen. Every decision was also a negotiation.

My role

I translated research and business needs into end-to-end flows and interfaces, built the components to hold them together, and handed them off to Engineering.

Decisions and tradeoffs

  1. Organise by what people do

    Chose
    A task-first home
    Over
    A product-first home

    Customers think “pay a bill,” not “open the payments module.” Structuring around tasks matched how people actually arrive.

  2. Fewer, clearer actions

    Chose
    A short list of primary actions
    Over
    Feature parity on day one

    Everything on the first screen meant nothing was findable. We chose a few actions to do well and gave the rest a clear place.

  3. Components built for the bank’s constraints

    Chose
    A reusable system
    Over
    One-off screens

    With many teams shipping, consistency had to be built in. A shared set of components made the good decision the easy one.

How it evolved

  1. Everything on the first screen

    The existing home gave every product and offer a tile. Complete, and a lot to read before doing anything.

  2. Grouped by what people do

    Regrouping around tasks removed most of the searching, even before the visual design changed.

  3. A home built around a few jobs

    The new home leads with balance, three primary actions and recent activity. The rest sits one clear step away.

What shipped

  • Task-first home with primary actions
  • Redesigned transfer and payment flows
  • Component library handed off for build

Outcome

Routine tasks took fewer steps to reach.

Teams had one set of components to build with instead of many.
