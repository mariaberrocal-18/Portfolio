Making a growing platform easier to navigate

A platform that grew module by module needed a structure organised around what people are trying to do, not how it was built.

Company
Clients
  • The world’s largest independent beverage bottler and a global FMCG leader.
  • The world’s largest brewer and a leading global beverage corporation.
  • A Fortune 500 consumer goods leader with omnipresent global brands.
Role
Senior Product Designer
Scope
Information architecture, navigation model, rollout
Worked with
Product, Engineering

Interfaces are reconstructed to respect client confidentiality.

The problem

As the platform grew, navigation grew with it: one entry per module, added as each shipped. The structure mirrored how the product was built, not how people work.

Why it was hard

Existing users had muscle memory. Roles used different parts of the product. Modules were at different levels of maturity. Any change had to help new users without losing the old ones.

My role

I led the information architecture and the navigation model, and shaped the rollout with Product and Engineering so the change could land in steps.

Decisions and tradeoffs

  1. Organise around questions, not modules

    Chose
    Groups based on what people are doing
    Over
    One entry per module

    People arrive with a question, such as “where are we at risk?” or “what is our plan?”. The structure should meet them there.

  2. Shallow and persistent

    Chose
    A persistent primary nav with contextual secondary
    Over
    A deep tree

    People always know where they are, and the deeper options only appear when they matter.

  3. Ship in steps

    Chose
    An incremental rollout
    Over
    A single big switch

    Familiar paths stayed where people expected them while the new structure took hold.

How it evolved

  1. A sprawl of entry points

    Mapping the existing product showed how many places a single task could start, and how few of them connected.

  2. Grouped by intent

    Collapsing the sprawl into a handful of groups, each named for a question people ask, gave the structure its first clear shape.

  3. One persistent navigation

    The final model keeps primary navigation stable and reveals secondary options in context.

What shipped

  • New information architecture
  • Persistent primary navigation with contextual secondary
  • Workspace and facility switcher

Outcome

New users found their way without a guide.

The structure had room for new modules without a redesign.
